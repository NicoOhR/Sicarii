The tech sector is entirely incoherent as a product class. The string which ties
together unrelated corporations which are regarded as the technology sector is
primarily a set of cultural norms and cost curves which are unique to it. This
is not an interesting observation. The product of *all* technology companies is
*not* code. I am not claiming that "coding was never the hard part". What I want
to establish before a more quantitative breakdown is that the technology is the
always the means and never the end. Google is an advertising company, Meta is an
addiction conglomerate, the budding canker sores of "exchanges" are gambling
bookies without the hats. Uber's primary product is arbitrage on the value of
labour, they are Pinkertons with two deployed Kafka instances. 

The members of the tech sector which do not sell to consumers and rather market
to other businesses, are arguably parts of the industry which truly sell
technology as a product. Even then, the B2B SaaS model relies on some underlying
asset that can be capitalized on for profit. Salesforce owns the digital real
estate where customer data lives; it is a landlord business, and its pricing
reflects switching costs rather than the quality of the technology. This is to
say that the actual value proposition of the technology sector is not how good
their software is, but their ability to leverage a particular class of asset.
What unites these companies is that the assets which they trade on require
a high fixed cost and a marginal cost of replication, which produces similar
strategic problems for the cohort. It is conceptually incorrect to analyze the
balance sheets of any company classified in tech in the same way one could
analyze any other sector. These companies sell different products to different
customers and on completely different time tables. The tech sector is only
coherent on the level of capital structure.

Hardware companies, which are often included in the tech sector a la ETFs like
QQQ or XLK, make even the capital structure relation dubious. Each piece of
hardware produced retains the high fixed cost of design but also has
a significant marginal cost. This discrepancy is resolved specifically for the
subject of this report, Nvidia; Nvidia is fabless, their moat is switching cost
from their industry standard CUDA ecosystem. Nvidia is the purest expression of
the tech capital structure that I am aware of. It has become clear that Nvidia
has become the engine behind the massive capex cycle that the market has seen in
the last two years. Their customers, Google, Meta, OpenAI, etc. absorb Nvidia's
output in an attempt to accelerate their own profit drivers, maybe cynically,
Nvidia in turn acts as the guarantor of their customers purchases, as well as
customers in and of themselves to the same companies. This report seeks to test
if NVIDIA's reinvestment into it's own customers overstates the quality of the
reported earnings, and if current market evaluation depends on that
exaggeration.
